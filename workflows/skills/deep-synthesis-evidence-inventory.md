# Deep Synthesis: Evidence Inventory

Use this skill when the corpus needs a usable evidence inventory before
mechanism or failure synthesis begins.

## Trigger

Use when you need to turn a broad research corpus into a structured inventory
that can support multi-agent synthesis without double counting or misrouting
evidence.

## Inputs

Bring:

- the frozen corpus inventory;
- source, trajectory, informal, and eval route maps;
- any intake metadata or prep notes;
- known stale or superseded prep artifacts;
- the first-case-study slate or similar prioritization notes.

## Workflow

1. Inventory the evidence classes first.
2. Bind paths and artifacts to stable evidence dimensions.
3. Separate direct evidence, summaries, and low-confidence signals.
4. Split confidence where one bucket contains mixed credibility.
5. Route the corpus toward the first deep-synthesis priorities.

## Outputs

The artifact should include:

- the evidence inventory paths or path groups;
- a trajectory priority list;
- a codebase or system priority list;
- an eval priority list when relevant;
- source-type notes and confidence notes;
- malformed or missing evidence notes;
- first-case-study recommendations;
- stale or superseded prep artifacts.

## Validation Checklist

- evidence classes are explicit;
- trajectories, code, evals, and informal sources are not collapsed together;
- direct evidence is separated from inference;
- confidence labels are fine-grained enough for mixed-credibility material;
- the inventory is usable for deep synthesis, not just a scope note.

## What Not To Do

- do not start from prose elegance;
- do not leave the corpus as one undifferentiated bucket;
- do not pretend intake metadata is more complete than direct inspection;
- do not smuggle final claims into the inventory layer.
