# Synthesis And Adjudication

Use this skill when multiple analyses need to be turned into one public-safe
synthesis, case study, or decision memo.

The purpose is to make the evidence ladder explicit, preserve contradictions,
and avoid smoothing uncertainty into a fake consensus.

## Governing Question

> What survives after we separate evidence, inference, contradiction, and
> withheld material?

## When To Use

Use this skill for:

- research synthesis;
- wave closeout or artifact-level adjudication;
- public case-study drafting;
- evidence-frontier summaries;
- claim acceptance or rejection against a curated source set.

## Synthesis Workflow

### 1. Build An Evidence Inventory

List the source classes first:

- result rows and scoreboards;
- deterministic outputs;
- traces, logs, or receipts;
- source files or docs;
- public handoffs or review notes;
- missing artifacts and known blind spots.

### 2. Separate Claim Types

Keep these distinct:

- observed facts;
- inferred causal explanations;
- hypotheses;
- contradictions;
- unresolved questions.

### 3. Preserve The Coverage Frontier

Note both sides of the frontier:

- what was read and can be cited;
- what was not read but still matters;
- what is deliberately withheld from the public artifact.

### 4. Adjudicate Claims

For each major claim, decide:

- accepted;
- accepted with warnings;
- rejected;
- unresolved.

Do not force confidence if the evidence is thin.

### 5. Write The Public-Safe Synthesis

The output should be readable by a reviewer who does not have the raw private
history.

Useful sections:

- executive conclusion;
- evidence ladder;
- accepted claims;
- contradictions and warnings;
- open questions;
- publication boundary;
- next slice.

## What Good Adjudication Protects Against

- fake consensus;
- overclaiming readiness or leadership;
- smuggling private details into public prose;
- collapsing incompatible evidence classes;
- confusing "not yet read" with "not relevant".
