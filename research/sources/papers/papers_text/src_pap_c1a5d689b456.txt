CANONICAL LATTICES AND INTEGER RELATIONS ASSOCIATED TO
RATIONAL FANS
RIZWAN JAHANGIR
Abstract.We propose a canonical local-to-global lattice theory for rational fans. We define the
ray latticeLrays(Σ)and therelation latticeL rel(Σ)as invariants functorial under fan isomorphisms.
We introducestar-local relation lattices, defined via the relation lattice of the localized quotient fan,
which capture the linear dependencies visible within local neighborhoods. We define acodimension
filtrationon the global relation lattice and prove a generation theorem: the global lattice is gen-
erated by local relations supported on the stars of cones of codimension at least 1. This filtration
is sensitive to the facial structure ofΣ; explicit examples and a conjecture suggest that subdivi-
sions can only preserve or lower filtration depths, distinguishing fans with different combinatorial
topologies.
1.Introduction
Toric geometry relies heavily on the combinatorial data of a rational fanΣin a latticeN.
Standard computational approaches often depend on extrinsic choices, such as a fixed basis forN
or a specific ordering of the rays. In computational applications, such as the search for minimal
relations, this dependence can obscure intrinsic geometric properties. While the geometry of a
toric variety is determined locally by affine charts (cones) glued together, the standard description
of the integer relations among rays, specifically the kernel of the ray matrix, is inherently global.
To our knowledge, there is no systematic framework in the literature that decomposes this global
lattice into local sub-lattices associated with the fan’s topology in the manner pursued here.
In this paper, our primary aim is to develop a theory of integer lattices that is canonical in
a rigorous categorical sense. By canonical, we mean that our constructions are invariant under
the automorphisms of the lattice preserving the fan. For example, ifΣis acted upon by a lattice
automorphism, the filtration levels defined in this paper are preserved. Furthermore, under a
refinement (subdivision) of the fan, the structures behave monotonically, allowing for stability
analysis in resolution of singularities.
Our framework fits into the landscape of combinatorial algebraic geometry but distinctively
differs from existing structures. The Divisor Class Group, described by the exact sequence0→
M→Z Σ(1) →Cl(X)→0, captures global relations [3, 5], but it does not explain how these
relations are assembled from local geometric data. Similarly, while the lattice idealIΣ encodes
integer relations [4], standard Gröbner basis theory treats the rays as a vector configuration, often
ignoring the fan’s facial structure. Matroid theory captures linear dependencies via circuits [8],
but standard matroid theory "forgets" the face poset structure of the fan, failing to distinguish
between a "local" relation supported on a cone’s star and a "global" relation spanning topologically
disjoint cones.
The goal here is not to replace these theories, but to supply a localization principle for relation
lattices that is invisible to purely global constructions. This philosophy builds directly on the
local-to-global constructions developed for intersection cohomology in [1] and [2].

Our main contributions are fourfold. First, we establish the ray latticeLrays and relation lattice
Lrel as functors on the category of rational fans (under isomorphisms). Second, we define star-local
lattices via the quotient fan, providing an intrinsic geometric definition. Third, we introduce a
canonical filtrationF kLrel based on the codimension of supporting stars. Finally, we establish
that the global relation lattice is generated by proper stars (Theorem 4.2). We note that this
paper focuses on structural properties; we do not discuss algorithms for computing short vector
bases (SVP) or Markov bases, nor do we address the computational complexity of determining the
filtration levels for high-dimensional fans.
The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 defines the category of rational
fans and the local geometric operations. Section 3 constructs the global ray and relation lattices.
Section 4 introduces the core novelty: the intrinsic star-local lattices and the codimension filtration,
culminating in the Local-to-Global Generation Theorem. Section 5 discusses the simplified struc-
ture in the simplicial case and functoriality under refinements, and Section 6 provides illustrative
examples.
2.Preliminaries and Notation
We begin by establishing the precise categorical and geometric foundations required to define
our invariants. Unless otherwise stated, all fansΣare assumed to berational,strongly convex, and
finite. The underlying latticeNis a free abelian group of rankn. We generally assume fans are
essential (not contained in a subspace), though the theory adapts to the general case.
To make our canonical claims precise, we define the categoryRatFan. The objects ofRatFan
are pairs(N,Σ), whereNis a free abelian group of finite rank andΣis a rational fan inN R. A
morphismϕ: (N,Σ)→(N ′,Σ ′)is a group homomorphismϕ:N→N ′ such that for every cone
σ∈Σ, there exists a coneσ ′ ∈Σ ′ withϕ R(σ)⊆σ ′. See [5, Ch. 1] for a standard treatment of fan
morphisms. In this paper, functoriality is verified primarily for isomorphisms and for subdivisions
(refinements) within a fixed lattice. For general morphisms inRatFan, additional care is required
because rays may map to non-primitive vectors or collapse to the origin.
The combinatorial topology of a fan is captured by the stars of its cones. Forτ∈Σ, thestar
isStar(τ) ={σ∈Σ|τ⪯σ}. Stars encode the local face structure aroundτ. Our local relation
lattices will be defined using the geometry of these stars. We letΣ(1)τ denote the set of rays
contained in cones ofStar(τ). We denote the codimension of a cone bycodim(τ) =n−dim(τ).
The construction of quotient fans associated to stars is standard; see [5, Section 3.1] or [6, Section
1.3].

6.Illustrative Examples and Comparison
6.1.Example 1: The Projective Plane.ConsiderN=Z 2. LetΣbe the fan forP 2, with rays
generated byv 1 = (1,0),v 2 = (0,1), andv 3 = (−1,−1).
•Global:The unique primitive relation is(1,1,1), corresponding tov 1 +v 2 +v 3 = 0.
•Local (τ=v 1):The star of rayv 1 contains the conesσ12 andσ 13. The rays involved are
{v1, v2, v3}. Thus, the relation(1,1,1)is supported onStar(v 1).
•Result:The global relation is generated at levelk= 1(codimension 1).

6.3.Example 3: Nontrivial Filtration Sensitivity.LetN=Z 3. Consider four rays
u1, u2, u3, u4 that form a circuitu 1 +u 2 +u 3 +u 4 = 0, where any three are linearly indepen-
dent.
(1)FanΣ A (Tetrahedral):Let the cones be the faces of a tetrahedron involving these rays.
Then every subset of 3 rays forms a cone. The relationu1 +u 2 +u 3 +u 4 = 0is supported on
the union of stars of these 3-cones (rays). However, there is no single 2-cone (codimension
1) containing all 4 rays. Thus, this relation might appear inF2 but not necessarilyF1.
(2)FanΣ B (Pyramidal):Suppose we introduce a new rayu 0 and triangulate such that
u1, u2, u3, u4 are all neighbors ofu0. Then the relation is supported onStar(u0), which is
codimension 0 (ifu0 is a ray in 3D). This is trivial.
(3)The Crucial Case:Consider a fanΣ C whereu 1, u3 are opposite in a quadrilateral cone
σwhich is subdivided by adding a diagonal. If the diagonal connectsu1, u3, thenu 2 and
u4 are separated. A relation involving all four cannot be supported on the star of the
diagonal ray (which only seesu1, u3). It must be assembled from relations on the stars of
the boundary rays.
This demonstrates that the filtration level reflects the "distance" between rays in the adjacency
graph of the fan.

6.4.Comparison with Known Invariants.
•Primitive Collections:Batyrev’s primitive collections [7] define relations corresponding
tominimalnon-faces. OurF 1 relationscorrespondtoprimitivecollectionsofsize2(adjacent
rays) or 3 (around a dimension 1 edge in 3D). Higher filtration levels capture primitive
collections that are "topologically large."
•Castelnuovo-Mumford Regularity:The filtration depth is analogous to the regularity
of the toric ideal, measuring the "complexity" of the syzygies.
7.Conclusion
We have presented a coordinate-free framework for the integer linear algebra of rational fans.
The main theorem establishes that the global relation latticeLrel(Σ)is generated by intrinsic local
relations supported on proper stars. The associated codimension filtrationF k provides a new
structural invariant measuring the geometric complexity of relations. Future work will investigate
the computational utility of this filtration for optimizing lattice reduction algorithms in toric
settings.
